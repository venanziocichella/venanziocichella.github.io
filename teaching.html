Teaching

I teach control theory at the University of Iowa, from the required undergraduate course to a PhD course on cooperative autonomous systems that I created.

ME:3600 Control of Mechanical Engineering Systems

Required undergraduate course · Fall semesters since 2020

An introduction to feedback control for mechanical engineers: modeling dynamic systems, transfer functions, stability, root locus, and frequency-response design with Bode plots. I developed new course materials, including a textbook, lecture slides, homework, exams, and lab activities.

ME:4120 Advanced Linear Control Systems

Senior and graduate elective · Taught since 2018

State-space methods for linear systems: modeling and simulation, controllability and observability, stability, state feedback, state observers, and observer-based control. I developed this course; it is required for the Robotics and Autonomous Systems focus area.

ME:6115 Cooperative Autonomous Systems

PhD course (formerly ME:5115) · Taught since 2019

A course I created on autonomy for single and multiple vehicles: dynamic modeling of autonomous systems, stability and nonlinear control for path following, and optimal control and numerical optimization for motion planning. In lab sessions in the Cooperative Autonomous Systems Lab, students apply these ideas to make ground robots and drones navigate an indoor space on their own.

Course materials

Tutorials and examples from these courses are on GitHub in Classic-and-Advanced-Linear-Controls:

  • Classic: mass-spring-damper, root locus, Bode plots, inverted pendulum, and a high-wire artist example
  • ALCS: Simulink introduction, controllability and observability, stability, state feedback, state observers, and observer-based control
  • CAS: modeling, planning, and tracking for autonomous vehicles
